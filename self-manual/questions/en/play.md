# Let's play: games and formats

The fun lives in the game design, not in extra exclamation marks. Play one at a time. Afterwards, drop the interesting answers into the pocket, then decide whether to gently unpack or go deep (see the three depth levels in `interviewer.md`). Answers from games still need their OK before going into the manual. Don't suggest games when they're feeling low.

## This or that, rapid fire
Throw out 5–8 pairs, ask for gut picks, then ask "why?" about the one or two most interesting.
- Rainy day in / rainy day walk
- Small lively get-together / quiet time alone
- Planned ahead / figure it out as you go
- Old favorite / new place you've never tried
- Early bird / night owl
- Small and cozy room / big and airy room
- Listening / talking
- The rush of finishing / the flow of doing
- Being needed / being understood
- Zoning out with someone / zoning out alone
- Window seat / corner seat
- Say it all at once / talk it through slowly
- A surprise / exactly what you wanted
- Summer nights / winter mornings
- One big goal / lots of small ones
- Told in advance / surprised on the spot

## Fill in the blank
- "My perfect Saturday is: ____."
- "Every time I ____, I feel alive again."
- "The moment I hear ____, I want to escape."
- "The little hobby I least like admitting is ____."
- "If I had a day entirely to myself, I'd ____."
- "The last time I thought 'that was money well spent', I'd bought ____."
- "Nobody knows this, but I'm actually scared of ____ situations."
- "As long as I have ____, I can get through even a rough day."
- "I used to love ____, and somehow stopped."

## What if
- Tomorrow you get a bonus Saturday that's entirely yours. Nobody needs you and nothing has to get done. How do you spend it? (Start broad, then ask: when do you wake up, what's the first thing you do, what's lunch, who are you with in the evening?)
- Someone gives you money that has to be spent within a week, only on things that make you happy. What do you do with it?
- A month of total freedom, no one to answer to. Where do you go first?
- You can delete one thing from your current life and add one thing. What are they?
- You can relive any day from your past, just to experience it again without changing anything. Which day?
- You're sent to live in an unfamiliar city for a month with only three things. What do you bring, and where do you go on day one?

## Mystery box
Have them pick a number from 1 to 9. Each number is an area on the category map (see `en/voice.md`). Pick a ☀️ question from that area. If they don't like it, they can "swap" with no reason needed.

## Character sketches
- "Describe the friend you feel most at ease with in three words."
- "If your ideal day were a movie, what genre would it be? Which scenes are in it?"

## Photo roll
- "Scroll your camera roll from the last three months. Which photos give you a 'that was a good day' feeling?"
- "What's the thing you have the most photos of? What does that say you care about?"

## Today in one line
- "Was there a moment today you'd like to keep? What was it?"
- "What small thing made you a little happy today?"

## Your user manual: gadget edition
Pretend they're a gadget and write a few lines of the product manual together. It's silly, and real preferences fall out of it:
- "Optimal operating conditions": where does this device run best?
- "Charging": how do you recharge this thing?
- "Low battery warning": what signs show up when it's running out? (Fewer words, craving sweets, not replying…)
- "Do not": what makes it crash?
Then pick one or two of the best lines and ask, "Is there a recent example of this one?"

## Time capsule
- "Send a message to yourself a year ago: what did you figure out about yourself this year?"
- "If you a year from now read today's manual, which entry do you think they'd change?"

## Three moments
- "Introduce your last month in three moments: one happy, one tiring, one hard to name. One sentence each."
- Take the one they feel most about and gently unpack it for a round or two.

## This vs that: the real version
Not hypotheticals, but two things they actually lived, side by side:
- "Your last two weekends: which was better, and what was the difference?"
- "Your last two takeout orders: which one was more satisfying?"
- "This week versus last week: which was more tiring?"
Good for comparing without going too deep.

## Top three
- "The top three noodle dishes you've ever had?"
- "The three most comfortable places you've been, ranked."
- "The three best small purchases this year."
Then ask, "What earned number one its spot?" That usually reveals the ingredients.

## Reverse interview
Ask them to write "the one question someone should ask if they wanted to really know me". Rewrite it as a concrete scene question and hand it back for them to answer. The question they write is itself a clue: what they most want to be asked is often what they care about. Good when they're a bit stuck and want a change of pace.
